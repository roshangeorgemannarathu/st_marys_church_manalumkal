Our Story

Where a small chapel became a spiritual home.

The history of Manalumkal Church is deeply connected with the land, the school, the families and the faith of the people of Poovathilappu.

St. Mary's Church Manalumkal, Kottayam

Located east of Kottayam — the city often described as the “Land of Letters and Culture” — lies the beautiful and fertile village of Poovathilappu. Blessed with greenery and rich agricultural land, the area has long been home to farming families and a close-knit community.

In earlier days, when transport facilities were scarce, Poovathilappu served as a resting place for people travelling on foot from Pala, Muttuchira, Paika, Kodungoor and nearby regions. Tradition says the name Poovathilappu came from a large Poovam tree, beneath which stood a resting platform called “Ilappu”, beside a pond.

As the area developed into an administrative centre of Akalakkunnam, schools, offices, trade centres and other institutions emerged. Alongside the growth of education came a deep desire to nurture faith. On the elevated sandy land known as Manalumkal — “land of sand” — a small chapel was eventually established near the school.

“That small chapel gradually became the spiritual heart of the region — a place where knowledge, prayer and community grew together.”

THE BEGINNINGS OF MANALUMKAL CHURCH

In 1924, under the leadership of Rev. Fr. Mathai Moongamackal, St. Aloysius English Middle School began functioning and was upgraded to a High School in 1949. Recognizing the spiritual needs of the children, Fr. Mathai and the parents sought to establish a dedicated place for meditation and worship.

In 1955, under the chairmanship of Fr. Joseph Periyappuram, the school committee decided to build a chapel. On November 5, 1955, secretary A.L. Joseph Maniyangatt was entrusted with purchasing the land. Rev. Fr. Zacharias Poovathingal later began the construction.

The foundation stone of the chapel, dedicated to Our Lady of Fatima, was laid in December 1956 by Mar Sebastian Vayalil, the first Bishop of Palai. With generous cooperation from local people through funds, timber and voluntary labour, the church was blessed and inaugurated on December 8, 1958, the Feast of the Immaculate Conception.

Under Rev. Fr. Michael Thekkel, the chapel was rebuilt and became a permanent chapel with a resident priest from April 1962. Daily Mass and regular religious services began, and in June 1965 Sunday School was started for children from nearby parishes.

The years that followed were marked by steady growth. Land was acquired, the church surroundings were developed, a sacristy and compound walls were built, a church gate and water facilities were added, and the community increasingly felt the need for an independent parish.

In 1984, the Manalumkal chapel was officially elevated to a substation (Kurisupalli) of Kanjiramatam Parish. On May 2, 1985, Rev. Fr. Joseph Eenthanal became its first vicar. On December 3, 1986, 167 families jointly submitted an application seeking independent parish status.

After a commission studied the proposal, Bishop Joseph Pallikkaparambil issued Decree No. 115 on February 15, 1987, officially establishing Manalumkal Parish. Rev. Fr. Joseph Eenthanal was appointed its first vicar, with 215 families incorporated into the new parish.

Since then, the parish has grown through prayer, unity and service. Ward prayer meetings, social welfare initiatives, house construction assistance, parish organizations and community programs have strengthened the bonds among its families.

In April 2005, the parish general body decided to construct a new church building. Through a Church Construction Fund, monthly adoration offerings and generous contributions from parishioners, the long-cherished dream became a reality.

The new St. Mary's Church, Manalumkal was solemnly inaugurated on December 26, 2010, by Mar Joseph Kallarangatt — a historic milestone in the parish's journey of faith and togetherness.
